The biggest thing in this update is the voices. The first version used simple text-to-speech that sounded flat and robotic. It did the job, but it was never going to scare anyone. I've now re-voiced every line of dialogue in both English and Russian, and it's a completely different game to play.

**Vetrov is me**

Vetrov is the man you play, sent into the sanatorium to find out why the station went silent. He's now voiced by a clone of my own voice. I recorded samples, trained a voice on them, and listened to myself narrate a horror game I wrote. It's a very strange experience, and I recommend it.

It also works in English. I'm a native Russian speaker, so English Vetrov has my accent, and I decided to keep it. He's a Soviet signalman walking through an abandoned Soviet facility, so it fits better than any polished narrator voice could.

**The rest of the cast**

-   **Major Gromov** speaks to you over the radio. He's now older and firmer, and he knows more than he tells you.
-   **The woman** you hear in the building has a soft, silky voice that is much creepier than a scream.
-   **The 1976 tapes** have a new voice for the scientist who recorded them, still on the same worn-out reel.
-   **The children** have new voices over the phone, in whispers, and on the air.

The other characters were generated with ElevenLabs. I kept all the audio treatment from the original version: the radio crackle, the tape hiss and the old telephone line. The new voices sit inside the world rather than on top of it.

**Also in this update**

-   The whole game is darker. Lamps are dimmer, the shadows between them are deeper, and the torch actually matters now.
-   The dead lamp at the end of the first corridor now stays dead.
-   A certain someone no longer waits for you at the bottom of the stairs before she's meant to. When you meet her now, it's on purpose.
-   A strange boarded window that had drifted onto the wall above the classroom door is gone.
