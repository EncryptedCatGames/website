An update about controllers, the Steam Deck, and something I've been experimenting with.

**🎮 A proper controller test**

The controller test now opens in its own window. Go to **Settings → Controller → Test controller…** and you'll see a drawing of your controller:

-   buttons **light up** as you press them;
-   each stick has a dot that **moves with it**, inside a ring showing your dead zone;
-   the triggers **fill** as you squeeze them;
-   the labels follow your controller: Xbox, PlayStation or Steam Deck.

There's also a **Test vibration** button. To close the window, **hold B** (or press Esc), so a quick B press can still be tested like any other button.

**🕹 Steam Deck and touch-screen laptops get the full game**

The Steam Deck has a touchscreen, and the game used to think it was a phone: on-screen buttons and lighter phone graphics. Now any computer with a touchscreen, including the Deck, Surface tablets and touch laptops, starts with **full desktop graphics and controls**. The on-screen touch buttons only appear if you actually touch the screen.

On the Deck, the game also understands its built-in controls directly, so buttons aren't mixed up. If the Deck's controls arrive as a keyboard and mouse instead (Steam does this in Desktop Mode), the game shows a short tip on how to fix it.

**🧪 Experiment: The Cradle as a native app**

Behind the scenes, I've been trying something: wrapping the game as a **native app**, so it runs outside the browser. I got it running on my **Steam Deck**, in both Desktop Mode and Game Mode, added to Steam with its own controller layout, and it works great: full screen, full graphics, the Deck's controls just work. A **Windows** version is built too, and I'm testing it next.

It isn't released yet. I want it rock solid first. In a future update I plan to offer **downloads for Steam Deck / Linux and Windows** right here on itch, next to the browser version. If you'd play it that way, tell me in the comments!
